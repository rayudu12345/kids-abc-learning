# Offline audio and animal motion

## Recorded audio

The app bundles 85 voice and animal recordings in `assets/audio/` (about 0.8 MB total): all 26 US English letter names, many basic words, numbers 1–10, short instruction words, colors/shapes where available, and six real animal/nature sounds. These are downloaded and converted assets, not device-generated speech saved as recordings.

Every recording has its author, original source URL, license, and conversion notes in `assets/audio/attribution.json`. `assets/audio/LICENSE.md` explains the applicable attribution/share-alike terms. Audio files retain their individual licenses. The cat, dog, and garden-bird sounds are short, quieter excerpts. Garden birds are not labeled as parrots.

The audio library shows **Recording** or **Device voice** for each basic word/name and offers visible credits. Additional guidance and unavailable clips still use device speech. Download an offline English voice for complete spoken guidance without connectivity.

Current unavailable examples: **grapes, orange, X-ray, newt, pink, oval**. Not every item is currently used in a lesson (pink is not a target in the six-color game). These use device speech when requested. Longer instruction sentences also use device speech; short instruction words such as listen, count, match, find, and trace have recordings.

## Phonics scope

The starter activity introduces six common **continuous initial sounds** (M, S, F, N, L, V) in pairs. It displays mouth-position guidance and plays a familiar animal word so the child can listen for its initial sound. Letter-name replay is a separate control. The app does not add an artificial “uh” to consonants or present a letter-name recording as a phoneme.

Dedicated recordings of isolated phonemes are **not bundled**. Word examples are clearly labeled as examples; newt currently uses device voice. Before expanding to stop consonants or vowel variations, obtain and review appropriate isolated-sound recordings with an early-literacy educator. English letters can have multiple sounds; the screen states that these are starter examples.

## Playback lifecycle

`audio/AudioController.ts` owns one active recording sequence. New requests stop the old clip and device speech, invalidate stale asynchronous requests, and release listeners/player resources. Navigation, mute, screen cleanup, and backgrounding stop playback. Clips have a timeout and a safe device-speech fallback. The audio plugin is configured for playback only, without microphone recording permission.

Automated tests check cancellation, stale request races, fallback handling, asset/license manifests, browser playback requests, and cleanup during navigation. They cannot establish the audible quality, volume, voice availability, or pronunciation on a physical Samsung tablet.

## Animal animations

`assets/animal-motion.png` is a project-generated sprite sheet with four poses for each of eight illustrated animals: cat, dog, elephant, fish, lion, parrot, rabbit, and zebra. `data/animalMotion.ts` defines row indices and frame looping. `AnimatedAnimal` applies gentle movement and a subtle breathing scale. `AnimalMotionSprite` blends neighboring poses over at most 60 milliseconds rather than abruptly replacing the image. Both pose timers and blend/body animations stop on unmount/background and reduced-motion mode rests at pose zero.

These are realistic-style illustrated characters animated in 2D, not live animal footage, skeletal 3D models, or physically simulated motion. The 24 animal drawing outlines remain simplified tracing artwork. Not all 24 outlines have realistic motion sprites.

## Regenerating audio

Run `python scripts/fetch-audio.py` with FFmpeg available. The script preserves cached clips, accepts explicit licenses, retains attribution, and backs off on HTTP 429 responses. Review every new source and recording, update missing-asset notes, format `audio/recordings.ts`, and run the asset tests. The app never fetches these URLs at runtime.

## Original balloon and kite effects

`assets/effects/pop.mp3` and `kite.mp3` are original procedural sounds generated by `scripts/generate-sky-effects.py` using Python and ffmpeg. They contain no third-party samples or voices and are separate from the 85 attributed human/animal recordings. See `assets/effects/README.md`. They play through the shared cancellation controller, respect mute, and are stopped when leaving or backgrounding the activity.

The enlarged alphabet animal scenes reuse this atlas with per-species timing, resting poses, modest body motion, and vector habitats. They do not add new photographs, recordings, or video. Alphabet pronunciation now appends the matching real call for cat, dog, elephant, lion and parrot. A separate replay button plays only that call and respects mute.

## Online memory animal photographs

`data/memoryPhotos.json` records every fixed photo URL, original Commons source page, creator, license and license URL for 32 animals. Runtime fetches these photos from Wikimedia over HTTPS. These are external photographs, not new bundled images or animations. Each board uses at most four unique photos. All selected photos are CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public domain; the activity's Photo credits panel attributes the current board's sources and licenses. Photos are resized for card display. The original photo licenses continue to apply.

The catalogue covers 11 pets/farm animals, 10 wild animals, seven water animals and four birds. Names are intentionally generic (for example, a golden retriever represents Dog). Photos and credits were reviewed on 16 November 2026. The application does not browse unfiltered image searches or hot-swap sources from a remote catalogue. New names without a bundled word recording use device English TTS; 85 recordings have individually documented attribution.

## Activity introductions and picture puzzles

Every activity introduction is displayed as text and can be spoken with the device English TTS voice. No new human instruction recordings are included. Install/download an English voice for offline narration; unavailable speech does not prevent reading or playing. Replay uses the shared audio controller, so it cancels prior playback and stops on leaving/backgrounding. Puzzle completion reuses the bundled animal-word clips. Puzzle pieces clip the existing animal atlas without new image assets or license changes.

## Reviewed animal call expansion — 27 November 2026

Three new short recordings add about 40 KB: elephant trumpet (CC0, தகவலுழவன்), lion roar in captivity (public domain, த*உழவன்), and vernal hanging parrot flight call (CC BY-SA 4.0, Sharadapte). Full source pages, original URLs, authors and licenses are in `assets/audio/attribution.json` and in-app credits. The parrot call is from a named parrot species; garden birds remain separately labeled. Converted parrot audio retains CC BY-SA 4.0.

`scripts/fetch-animal-calls.py` trims leading silence and limits excerpts to 2.5 seconds, converts mono 22,050 Hz/48 kbps MP3, normalizes to -23 LUFS with a -6 dB true-peak target, and adds brief fades at both ends. Animal playback volume is also reduced. `fetch-audio.py` runs this expansion last and rebuilds the complete registry; it can also be regenerated separately. No samples are downloaded by the installed app. Physical-device listening is still needed to assess comfort and speaker volume.

Fish, rabbit and zebra do not yet have verified bundled calls. Their animated alphabet scenes and recorded names remain available; the app does not invent calls or substitute a different animal. Word/phonics recording gaps above remain. Embedded source metadata is stripped from the three new MP3s; required attribution remains in the separate credits manifest.

These improvements smooth existing illustrated poses; they do not add live footage or 3D animation.
