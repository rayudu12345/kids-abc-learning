# Preschool feature references and scope

Reviewed on 16 November 2026 from the public Google Play descriptions:

- [Keiki Learning games for Kids](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=gen.tech.keiki.world.learning&hl=en): memory cards, letter/number learning, tracing, picture/rotation puzzles, food harvesting, cooking, spelling, and songs.
- [Baby World](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.iz.baby.world.kids.learning.games.balloon.popping.educational&hl=en): balloons, surprise eggs, coloring, sensory play, music, puzzles, sorting, and daily routines.

These descriptions are feature references, not evidence of hands-on testing. The app uses its own existing artwork, audio and UI.

## Implemented in this expansion

- Animal memory pairs, expanded to 32 online photos and themed groups, with 2–4 pairs and matching rewards.
- Eight animal surprise eggs, with letter/name recordings and saved stars.
- Freehand coloring for 24 animal outlines, with brushes, eraser, undo and reversible clear.
- Separate Paint Studio with blank-canvas drawing, shapes/fill, undo/redo, persistent latest picture and PNG/SVG export.
- Animal picture puzzles with two/four pieces, whole-picture hints, tap/drag controls and saved rewards.
- Play and learn discovery hub with introductions for every option, spoken replay and safe game suggestions.

Existing alphabet, tracing, counting, colors/shapes, sound matching, balloons, stickers, and progress backup remain available. The expanded memory game needs internet for animal photos. Other bundled activities retain offline support.

## Remaining candidates

Fruit/vegetable sorting, musical play with original or licensed samples, and gentle daily-routine scenes are possible later additions. Full songs, stories, and more realistic sound games need suitable licensed recordings or original assets. Animal-coloring artwork stays only during its activity session. The separate Paint Studio now saves its latest picture locally and exports PNG/SVG; a multi-picture gallery remains a possible future improvement.

Keep activities short, use large targets and gentle retries, and introduce difficulty gradually for a three-year-old. Parental controls remain deferred; do not introduce accounts, ads, purchases, analytics or cloud services.
